# General Definitions and Cross-Contract Rules

> This document applies to every contract unless a specific wording says
> otherwise. Where a contract article and this document conflict, the contract
> article prevails.

### ART-D.1 — Statutory tariff

The reference tariff published by the statutory health insurance scheme for a
given act. A benefit expressed as a percentage of the statutory tariff includes
the amount already reimbursed by the statutory scheme. A benefit of 200% means the
total reimbursement across both schemes equals twice the statutory tariff, not
that the complementary scheme adds 200% on top.

### ART-D.2 — Coordinated care pathway

The member has a declared treating physician and was referred by them, or the act
falls within one of the published direct-access specialities. Consultations
outside the pathway attract a lower statutory reimbursement, and most contracts
reduce their own benefit accordingly.

### ART-D.3 — Waiting period

A period from the effective date of cover during which a specific benefit is not
available. Waiting periods run from the effective date of the member's cover, not
from the date of the contract generation.

### ART-D.4 — Calendar year and rolling period

A benefit expressed "per calendar year" resets on 1 January. A benefit expressed
"per 24-month period" runs from the date of the last reimbursement of that
benefit. The two are not interchangeable and a claim refused under one may be
payable under the other.

### ART-D.5 — Actively at work

The member is carrying out their normal occupation and is not on sick leave,
partial activity or any form of medical suspension, on the date concerned.

### ART-D.6 — Contracted facility

A healthcare facility that has signed an agreement with the insurer covering
tariffs and direct settlement. The list is published and updated quarterly.
Whether a facility is contracted affects the benefit level in several contracts.

### ART-D.7 — Accident

A sudden external event, independent of the member's will, causing bodily injury.
A condition that develops progressively is an illness for the purpose of every
contract, even where the member attributes it to their working conditions.

### ART-D.8 — Consolidation

The date from which a condition is no longer expected to improve. It marks the end
of incapacity benefits and the possible start of permanent impairment benefits.

### ART-D.9 — Own occupation

The occupation the member was carrying out at the date incapacity began. A member
is not required to accept retraining into a different occupation for cover to
continue, unless the specific contract says so.

### ART-R.1 — Migration between contract generations

A member who migrates to a newer generation is covered under the new wording from
the migration date. Time already served against a waiting period under the
previous generation counts towards the equivalent waiting period in the new one.

### ART-R.2 — Which generation applies to a claim

The wording in force at the date of the insured event applies, not the wording in
force at the date the claim is submitted. For a continuing incapacity, the event
date is the first day of incapacity.

### ART-R.3 — Dual cover

Where a member is covered by two complementary contracts, total reimbursement may
not exceed the expense actually incurred. The contract subscribed first settles
first.

### ART-R.4 — Territorial scope of statutory reimbursement

Where a contract benefit is expressed as a percentage of the statutory tariff and
no statutory reimbursement is payable, the benefit is calculated on the tariff
that would have applied had the act been carried out domestically.
