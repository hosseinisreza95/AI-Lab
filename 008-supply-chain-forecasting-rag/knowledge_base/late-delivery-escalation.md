# Late Delivery Escalation Procedure

## Scope

Applies to any store delivery whose projected arrival exceeds the promised date,
whether the shipment is still in transit or has already arrived late.

## Escalation Tiers

| Delay against promise | Tier | Who acts | Within |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 12 hours | 0 | No action. Logged only. | - |
| 12 to 24 hours | 1 | Store notified automatically; carrier tracking checked | 4 hours |
| 24 to 72 hours | 2 | Regional planner contacts carrier account manager | Same day |
| Over 72 hours | 3 | Supply chain manager; replenishment from nearest store considered | 4 hours |
| Any delay on a peak-season replenishment | 3 | Immediate, regardless of duration | 2 hours |

A shipment held at customs is always Tier 2 from the moment the hold is
recorded, because customs holds do not resolve on their own.

## What To Check Before Escalating

1. Confirm the promised date on the order, not the date the store expected. The
   two differ whenever a store placed an order after the daily cut-off.
2. Check the last carrier scan. A shipment with no scan for more than 18 hours is
   treated as lost in the network, not as late, and follows the lost-shipment
   procedure instead.
3. Check whether the lane has a known issue open. Escalating individually on a
   lane that already has an open incident duplicates work and slows the incident.
4. Check whether the destination store is at risk of stocking out. A late
   delivery into a store with three weeks of cover is a reporting matter; a late
   delivery into a store with two days of cover is an operational one.

## Substitution From Another Store

Permitted at Tier 3 where the receiving store has under 3 days of cover and
another store within the same region holds more than 14 days of cover for the
same category. Inter-store transfers must be raised as a transfer order, never as
an informal movement, or the stock record diverges from reality at both ends.

## Carrier Claims

A claim may be raised where the delay exceeds the contracted service level and
the cause is attributable to the carrier. Weather, customs and force majeure are
excluded under all current contracts. Claims must be raised within 21 days of
delivery with the tracking history attached.

## Repeat Lane Failures

Where the same lane and carrier combination misses its promise more than three
times in a rolling 30 days, the regional planner raises a lane review rather than
a per-shipment escalation. A lane review requires at least 20 shipments in the
window; below that the on-time rate is not a measurement.
