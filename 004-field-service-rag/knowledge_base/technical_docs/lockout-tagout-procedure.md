# Lockout / Tagout and Permit To Work Procedure

## Scope

Mandatory for any intervention that requires opening, entering, or mechanically
disturbing equipment that can store energy: pressure, electrical, thermal,
rotational, or chemical.

## Sequence

1. Raise the permit to work. No intervention starts without a signed permit.
2. Identify every energy source feeding the equipment. A single isolation is
   almost never sufficient; feed pumps typically have four, and compressors six.
3. Shut down through the normal control sequence, never by pulling a breaker.
4. Isolate: open breakers, close and chain block valves, insert spades where the
   procedure calls for them.
5. Apply personal locks. Every person working on the job applies their own lock
   to the hasp. The job is not locked out until the last person has locked on.
6. Dissipate stored energy: depressurise, drain, vent, allow to cool, discharge
   capacitors, release spring tension.
7. Verify zero energy at the point of work. Test the instrument you are using for
   the test first, on a known live source.
8. Tag with the permit number, the date, and the name of the permit holder.

## Removing a Lock

Only the person who applied a lock may remove it. A lock left on by an absent
technician is removed only under the documented lock removal procedure, with the
area authority and the shift supervisor both signing.

## Gas Testing Before Opening

For any hydrocarbon service, gas-test at the flange before breaking it, and test
again after opening. Purge with nitrogen until the reading is below 1 percent
LEL. Record both readings on the permit.

## Common Findings in Audits

- Isolation list incomplete, most often missing instrument air or a seal flush
  line.
- Verification step skipped because the pressure gauge read zero. A gauge can be
  isolated by its own root valve and read zero on a live line.
- Locks applied to a valve handwheel instead of a chain, so the valve can still
  be operated.
